What it does

Three things, all done in front of a switched-on amp.

The Spark 2 holds eight presets at a time. Everything else — the sounds you built, the ones you only need for one song, the ones you’re still trying out — has nowhere to live. This is that place.

Organise

As many presets as you like, with name, categories, notes and sound family. It reads them from the amp, imports the official app’s preset_backup.zip, exports and re-imports as JSON.

Reading the library from the amp again never wipes your work: it recognises the presets and rewrites only the sound, leaving tags, notes and order untouched.

Play

A Live view made to be used while you play, not for cataloguing: banks of eight big buttons, and an LED to recognise the sound at a glance.

A preset already loaded on the amp switches on instantly; one that isn’t there is sent whole in about a second.

Tweak

An effects-chain editor that works on the sound that’s playing: you turn a knob and the amp answers.

Before opening it always reads the real state back from the amp, because if you touched a knob on the panel the truth is there, not in the library. Nothing is saved until you ask.

Guide

Getting started

There’s nothing to install and nothing to sign up for. From a switched-on amp to your first organised library takes five minutes.

  1. Open the app and press «Connect»

    The browser asks which Bluetooth device to use: pick the Spark 2. It must be on and not connected to the official app — one master at a time. If you can’t find it in the list, that’s the first thing to suspect.

  2. Let it read the eight slots

    As soon as it’s connected it starts reading the presets you loaded into the amp by itself. It takes a few seconds. They appear at the top, labelled A1…B4 with the colours of the panel’s LEDs: red for bank A, green for bank B.

  3. Bring in everything else

    If you use the official app, export the preset_backup.zip from there and load it: your whole collection comes in at once. Otherwise the library grows as you go, with «Import current preset» every time you find something you like.

  4. Give things a name

    Categories for the style — the band, the song, the genre — and the family (clean, drive, acoustic) that becomes the colour you recognise the preset by without reading it. Notes are for what you discover by playing: which solo, which tuning, what to turn down when the room booms.

  5. Prepare the banks for the gig

    A bank is a set list: eight presets in the order you need them. The banks you make don’t touch the amp — the presets are loaded on the fly — so you can make as many as you like without the risk of overwriting the eight inside it.

  6. Install it, to have it without a network

    From the browser menu, «Install app» (or «Add to Home screen»). It becomes an icon, opens full screen and works offline: at a gig there’s no network, and it shouldn’t need one.

Before you start

Things to know

This project was born to solve a problem of mine and it works on my amp. The Spark 2 protocol isn’t documented anywhere: here it was reconstructed by observing, measuring and verifying on the hardware. Some things are worth knowing up front.

It doesn’t work on iPhone and iPad It needs Web Bluetooth, and on iOS every browser has to use Apple’s engine, which doesn’t have it. It’s not our choice and there’s no way around it. On Android, Windows, Mac and Linux, with Chrome or Edge, it works.
Writing to an amp slot can’t be undone Whatever was there before is lost. To try a preset without risking anything there’s the safe route, which plays it without touching the eight slots: it’s the one the app uses on its own.
The amp can freeze It happens with heavy commands, and then you have to unplug it. Nothing breaks, but if it happens in the middle of a song it’s a nuisance: better to find out at home than on stage.
The Jimi Hendrix pack effects stay silent They’re the Spark 2’s only paid content, and what unlocks them is a key the official app sends as soon as it connects. Connect the official app once and from then on they work, even after you’ve closed it. The app marks them with a JH badge, so you know before choosing one and not after hearing it silent.
The library lives in the browser No account and no server: none of your data leaves it. The flip side is that if you change browser or device it doesn’t follow you by itself. It exports to JSON, and for those who use it on several devices there’s a Dropbox sync — still with no server in between.
It has been tested on a single Spark 2 Mine. With another unit, or another firmware, I don’t know what happens.

Work in progress

The pedal

The live view, detached from the phone. An ESP32 takes the banks from the app over Bluetooth, keeps them in memory and then runs on its own: it connects to the Spark by itself and changes presets with your foot. Four footswitches for the sounds, one to switch halves of the bank without touching what’s playing.

It’s halfway there: the firmware runs on a bare board, the real device — footswitches, LEDs, display — isn’t built yet. But the interesting part has already been measured, and it isn’t bandwidth:

Loading a whole presetMeasured
from the phone, via Web Bluetooth1246 ms
from the ESP32, requesting a 7.5 ms interval326 ms
from the ESP32, once settled178–215 ms

The second the phone takes is sixteen round trips with the amp. Web Bluetooth doesn’t let you touch the connection interval; on the ESP32 you can, and that makes all the difference.

For developers

The Spark 2 protocol, documented

If you got here looking for how to talk to a Spark 2, this is probably the part you need. It’s all public, in the repository, with the raw captures and also the roads that turned out to be dead ends — so nobody walks them again. The documentation is in Italian.

  • GATT service 0xFFC0, write 0xFFC1 (only write without response), notifications 0xFFC2.
  • Effect commands want a trailing 0x00 byte: without it, the amp replies with a regular ack and applies nothing. It’s in no other source, and it’s a Spark 2 difference.
  • A preset’s chunks are 25 bytes, not 128: with 128 the Spark 2 drops the connection.
  • All the chunks of a preset go with the same sequence number, or the amp acknowledges them all and assembles none.
  • Playing a preset and saving it to a slot are two different routes, and the second needs a there-and-back hop that looks like decoration and isn’t.
  • The ack confirms receipt, not execution: the absence of errors is not a verification.
